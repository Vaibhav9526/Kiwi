KIWI is a passive network forensic framework that turns captured email traffic into a verifiable cryptographic verdict. It ingests PCAP files containing SMTP, IMAP, and POP3 traffic, reconstructs complete TCP streams and TLS handshakes, detects STARTTLS upgrades and downgrade attempts, extracts and analyzes X.509 certificates, and evaluates every session against a deterministic catalog of 37 security rules covering transport encryption, TLS versions, cipher suites, key exchange and forward secrecy, certificates, authentication, and downgrade indicators. The output is a reproducible posture score (A-F), findings prioritized worst-first with typed evidence (frame numbers, stream offsets, redacted excerpts), and exportable forensic reports in JSON, PDF, and HTML wrapped in a self-verifying SHA-256 envelope. An optional AI layer classifies risk, flags anomalous TLS behavior, and writes remediation guidance, but it explains rather than asserts: every AI statement must cite the deterministic finding keys it grounds in, and uncited claims are mechanically dropped. Uniquely, KIWI is also a working desktop mail client, so endpoint-level TLS observations fill the blind spots (such as TLS 1.3 encrypted certificates) that purely passive tools cannot see. It runs fully offline as a single binary, making it suitable for air-gapped SOC and forensic lab environments, and is released under MPL-2.0.
